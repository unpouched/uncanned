# Uncanned Sources

The Progress screen in Uncanned includes informational milestones counted from your last pouch. The references below discuss nicotine withdrawal and common triggers. Some focus on smoking or tobacco use rather than nicotine pouches specifically.

The dates are general reference points, not predictions for an individual. Symptoms and their duration vary. These references do not establish the effectiveness of Uncanned's taper algorithm or guarantee that a symptom will end on a particular day.

## 1 day: First full day without nicotine

Withdrawal symptoms start within hours of the last dose of nicotine.

- Cleveland Clinic, [Nicotine Withdrawal](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21587-nicotine-withdrawal): symptoms begin 4 to 24 hours after the last dose.
- MedlinePlus (U.S. National Library of Medicine), [Nicotine and tobacco](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000953.htm): symptoms appear within 2 to 3 hours.

## 3 days: Symptoms are often near their early peak

- Cleveland Clinic, [Nicotine Withdrawal](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21587-nicotine-withdrawal): symptoms are most intense on the second or third day and get a little better every day after the third day.
- MedlinePlus, [Nicotine and tobacco](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000953.htm): symptoms peak about 2 to 3 days after quitting.

## 4 days: Symptoms may begin to ease after the early peak

- Cleveland Clinic, [Nicotine Withdrawal](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21587-nicotine-withdrawal): the peak is on day two or three, and symptoms improve after the third day.

## 2 weeks: Early symptoms may be easing

- Smokefree.gov (U.S. National Cancer Institute), [Managing Nicotine Withdrawal](https://smokefree.gov/challenges-when-quitting/withdrawal/managing-nicotine-withdrawal): symptoms are usually strongest in the first days or weeks and tend to weaken over time. Experience differs between people.

## 4 weeks: Symptoms often improve over several weeks

- Cleveland Clinic, [Nicotine Withdrawal](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21587-nicotine-withdrawal): symptoms fade over days to three to four weeks.

## 3 months: Triggers may still prompt cravings

- Smokefree.gov, [Know Your Triggers](https://smokefree.gov/challenges-when-quitting/cravings-triggers/know-your-triggers): cravings are set off by emotions, routines and social situations, and you can plan for each kind.
- These sources describe ongoing triggers; they do not specify that all cravings after three months are caused only by triggers.

General information, not medical advice.
